Field Handbook

General guidance for supervisors and technicians. It does not replace the project specification, the manufacturer manual, the site permit-to-work or the current authority rules.

Pipe offsets (45° and other angles)

For an offset angle θ and a centre-line offset O:

  • Travel (centre to centre of the offset pipe) = O ÷ sin θ
  • Run (distance along the main line) = O ÷ tan θ
  • At 45°: travel = O × 1.414, run = O
  • At 60°: travel = O × 1.155, run = O × 0.577
  • At 30°: travel = O × 2, run = O × 1.732

Rolling offset: first find the true offset = √(vertical² + horizontal²), then use it as O above. Cut length = travel − the take-off of the two fittings.

Pipe bends and elbows
  • Arc length = π × R × angle ÷ 180
  • Tangent length = R × tan(angle ÷ 2)
  • Leg cut length = corner-to-corner leg − tangent at each bend
  • Long-radius elbow: R = 1.5 × nominal size. Short radius: R = 1 × nominal size. Confirm with the fitting you buy.

The Pipe sketch tool does these deductions for you through its cut list.

Mitre (segmented) bends
  • Two-piece 90° mitre: each cut face is at 45° to the pipe axis.
  • A mitre of total angle A with n cuts uses a cut face of A ÷ (2n) to the pipe axis.
  • Mark the cut line by wrapping paper around the pipe, or use a wrap-around template.
Cable tray and duct bends
  • Horizontal 90°: measure both leg lengths to the centre-line corner, then cut each leg at 45° across its width.
  • Riser or drop 90°: same layout, bent on edge. Keep the tray's minimum bending radius from the maker's data.
  • Tee: cut the main tray, then fit the branch. Support within the distance the maker specifies.
Hopper (square to round) layout
  1. Draw the plan view and the side view with the true height.
  2. Divide the circle into equal parts (for example 12) and join each point to its nearest square corner.
  3. Find each line's true length by the right-triangle method (height and plan length).
  4. Unfold the true-length triangles side by side to get the flat pattern.
  5. Add seam and flange allowances, then cut a trial piece in cheap sheet before the real one.
Cable sizing: the method
  1. Find the load current (see Calculators).
  2. Choose the cable type, installation method and ambient temperature.
  3. Apply the correction factors from the current standard to get the required table rating.
  4. Pick the cable whose table rating is at or above that value.
  5. Check voltage drop (Calculators) and the protective device short-circuit rating.

Current ratings and correction factors must come from the standard or the authority's document in force. This app does not hold them yet.

Insulation resistance test (Megger)
  1. Isolate, lock out and tag out. Prove the circuit dead with a tested voltage detector.
  2. Disconnect electronics, surge protectors and anything the test voltage can damage.
  3. Choose the test voltage from the equipment rating and the specification.
  4. Connect between conductors, and between each conductor and earth. Apply the test for the time in the procedure, usually 60 seconds.
  5. Record the reading and temperature, then discharge the circuit before touching it.
Continuity check
  1. Make the circuit dead and prove it.
  2. Touch the meter leads together and note the lead resistance, or use the null function.
  3. Test across the conductor. A low steady reading means continuity; an open reading means a break.
  4. For protective earth conductors, record the value against the specification.
4–20 mA loop check and HART
  • 0% = 4 mA, 25% = 8 mA, 50% = 12 mA, 75% = 16 mA, 100% = 20 mA.
  • Inject or simulate at 0, 50 and 100% and compare the reading at the control system.
  • A HART communicator needs about 250 Ω of loop resistance to talk to the transmitter.
  • Trim only after you record the as-found values, and record the as-left values.
Transformer turns-ratio test (CPC 100 or similar)
  1. Isolate and earth the transformer. Disconnect the bushings from the system.
  2. Connect the instrument to the HV and LV windings of each phase as the instrument manual shows.
  3. Test every tap position. Compare the measured ratio with the nameplate ratio at that tap.
  4. The deviation limit is commonly ±0.5% (IEC 60076-1). Confirm it against your specification.

Safety: always follow your company procedure, permit-to-work and the manufacturer manual. If you are not sure, stop and ask your supervisor.
